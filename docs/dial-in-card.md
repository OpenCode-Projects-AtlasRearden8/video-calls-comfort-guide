# The Family Dial-In Card

One page that answers the two questions that sink the most family calls: *"which app?"
*and "which link?"* Fill it in once, share it with everyone, or print it and stick it
on the fridge next to the emergency numbers. It's the video-call equivalent of the
phone-list card that used to live by the kitchen wall.

Copy this into a message to your family (or print this page and fill it in by hand —
the checkboxes are just decoration there; leave the blanks for pen).

---

### If it doesn't work, plan B is:

1. **Try ____________ instead** (a different app, or a plain phone call).
2. If video fails, **switch to sound only** — turn the camera off; the visit can
   continue beautifully on voices. See [troubleshooting](08-troubleshooting.md).
3. **Last resort:** call this number: ______________ (that's ______________'s phone).

### House rules we all agreed on

- ☐ Captions on by default ([chapter 3](03-captions.md))
- ☐ No recording, or ask before any note-taker bot joins ([chapter 6](06-ai-note-takers.md))
- ☐ Calls end while they're still sweet — short and warm beats long and tired
- ☐ Frozen screens are nobody's fault; keep talking, we'll hear you

---

## Why this tiny card works so well

- It removes the "wait, which app?" scramble that keeps older relatives from dialing in
  on time — and keeps younger relatives from starting three different calls.
- Plan B means a frozen screen is a two-minute detour, not an abandoned visit.
- The house rules mean decisions (captions, bots, call length) get made **once**, calmly,
  instead of every call, from scratch.

> Warm advice: fill it in *once* with the family, then never argue about apps again.
