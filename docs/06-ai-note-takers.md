# AI Note-Taker Bots: Consent, and Kind Wording for Both Sides

Somewhere in the last few years, most meetings quietly grew a new guest: an **AI
note-taker**. It's a bot — a piece of software that joins your video call, listens,
takes notes, and emails everyone a summary afterward. Names you'll see include Zoom AI
Companion, Microsoft Copilot in Teams, Google's Gemini "take notes for me," Otter,
Fireflies, Read AI, and Fathom. They're hugely useful in offices and increasingly
common in family life, board meetings, book clubs, and doctors'-adjacent appointments.

They also raise a simple human question: **did everyone on this call agree to be
recorded?** This chapter answers it kindly — for the person who wants the bot, and for
the person who doesn't.

## First, know what you're inviting

A note-taker bot can do all of these, depending on the tool:

- Join the call as its own participant (sometimes with a name like "Otter Notary" or
  "Copilot").
- Record the audio, or transcribe it live.
- Write a summary, action items, and sometimes a list of who said what.
- Keep all of that on somebody's servers — the bot company's, or your workplace's.

So when a bot joins, the honest framing is: **a recording is happening.** Not a scary
one, necessarily. But the same permission rules as a tape recorder apply — almost
everyone agrees asking first is the neighborly thing.

## The consent habit, in one sentence

Whoever invites the bot says so, out loud, at the start of the call:

> "Quick housekeeping — I've got our note-taker bot on so I don't miss anything.
> It transcribes the call and sends a summary after. Anybody rather it sit this one out?
> Just say so, no reason needed."

Then wait a beat, not three seconds. People are genuinely shy of objecting. The pause
is part of the invitation.

## How to say no thanks, warmly

If a bot joins and you'd rather it didn't, you can say it in one breath, no reasons
required. Pick whichever sounds like you:

- **Simple:** "Mind if we keep this one off the transcript? I'm happiest off the record."
- **Small request:** "Could the bot skip my bit? I'd rather this part stay between us."
- **Firm but soft:** "I'd rather not be recorded on this call. Can someone take notes by
  hand instead?"
- **Hospital-grade short:** "No recording today, please. Thanks."

And if you're the *host* hearing that request, the graceful response is two words:

> "Of course — turning it off."

Or, if the rest of the group wants notes: "No problem — we'll pause it for your part."
Nobody is wrong here. Recording-without-asking is the only wrong move.

## If you're the host: turning a bot off (the usual places)

- **Zoom AI Companion:** the host can turn it off in-meeting (the AI Companion menu on
  the toolbar) or in *Settings → AI Companion* before the meeting starts.
- **Teams / Copilot:** the meeting organizer can toggle recording and transcription in
  the meeting controls; org-level settings live with whoever administers Microsoft 365.
- **Google Meet / Gemini notes:** host controls under *Host controls → Notes*.
- **Third-party bots (Otter, Fireflies, Read AI, Fathom):** each has its own web
  dashboard with a settings page per meeting or per calendar. Everyone on the service
  with an account can also usually delete a transcript afterward — worth doing just to
  keep house tidy.

One extra mercy for everybody: after the call, the summary email is usually shared by
link. Forwarding it outside the meeting group is the same ethics as forwarding any
recording — ask first.

## When a bot is genuinely a good idea

To be fair to the little helpers — used with consent, they're wonderful at:

- Catching the action items from a busy committee or volunteer meeting.
- Giving a summary to the family member who couldn't join the reunion-planning call.
- Helping someone who processes better from reading than from live audio.
- Keeping a work meeting honest about who-owns-what-by-when.

The kindness isn't "never use bots." The kindness is **everyone knowing they're there.**
A visible bot everyone welcomed is a servant. A silent bot nobody noticed is an eavesdropper.

## A quiet privacy note

Recording tools and *medical* calls deserve extra caution: a doctor's visit through a
patient portal, or anything sensitive your family keeps among family. It's perfectly
normal — and increasingly expected — to say "no recording today" for exactly those
calls. The bots don't mind. They're bots.
