Glossary, in Plain English

Words about video calls, defined like a friend would explain them over the fence. Nobody uses all of these; the good ones to know are marked in the text of the book.

A

AI note-taker — A bot that joins a video call and takes notes, transcribes, and emails a summary. Common in offices; increasingly in book clubs. See chapter 6 for the consent etiquette.

Audio-only — A call with no video, just voices. The reliable fallback when internet is weak or someone's camera is having opinions.

B

Bandwidth — How much internet "room" your connection has for the moment. Live video uses a good slice of it. When the room runs low, video freezes first and sound survives — that's by design.

Bot — A small program that acts like a participant. In this guide, always means the note-taking kind. See: AI note-taker.

Browser tab / window — A tab is one page inside a browser; a window is a separate box containing its own tabs. When sharing your screen, share one window, never the whole screen. See: chapter 5.

C

Captions (CC) — The words of the conversation shown at the bottom of the screen, made live by the app. Turn them on freely; they help nearly everyone. Chapter 3.

Chat (in-call) — The little text messaging inside a call, handy for links, addresses, or "your mic is muted!" in a polite whisper.

D

Do Not Disturb / Focus mode — A device setting that stops notification pop-ups during a call. Consider turning it on before you share a screen.

E

Echo — Hearing your own voice back with a small delay, because someone's microphone is re-hearing their speaker. Fixed with headphones or muting the quiet ones. Chapter 8 has the kind walkthrough.

F

FaceTime — Apple's video calling, built in on iPhone/iPad/Mac. Works beautifully at long distance between Apple devices; can switch between audio and video mid-call.

Frozen squares — What it looks like when someone's video stalls: their face becomes a still photo, or moves a second at a time. Not anyone's fault; see chapter 8.

G

Google Meet — Google's browser-based video calling. Free with a Google account; runs right in the browser, which makes it a favorite for easy family calls.

H

Headphones / headset — Anything that plays sound close to your ears and, if it has a microphone, picks up only your voice. The single best gear purchase for calls, at any price. See: chapter 1.

Host — The person who "owns" a scheduled meeting. Hosts can mute people, turn captions on, remove note-taker bots, and stop anyone's screen share.

J

Join (a call) — Clicking the link or button that puts you in. That's the whole word.

L

Link — The web address (looks like https://…) that leads to a meeting. One tap and you're in the waiting room.

Live captions — Captions being written as people speak, by the app itself, which is why an occasional word turns into something odd. Forgive them; they try.

M

Meeting ID — The number that identifies a specific Zoom or Teams meeting.

Mic (microphone) — What hears you. Mute is its off switch. Push to Talk is its walkie-talkie mode (Zoom only). See: chapter 4.

Mute — Turning off your microphone while everything else keeps running. The mother of all good call manners. Chapter 4 is the full etiquette.

N

Note-taker (bot) — See: AI note-taker.

P

Passcode — A short word or number required to get into some meetings. Family calls rarely need one; work calls usually do.

Push to Talk — A Zoom setting: stay muted by default, hold the space bar to speak.

R

Raise hand — A button in most apps that makes a hand icon appear next to your name, for politely claiming a turn in a busy call.

Recording — Making a permanent copy of a call's audio (sometimes video). If anyone or any bot is recording, everyone should know before the call starts. This is the whole point of chapter 6.

S

Screen share — Showing something from your device to everyone on the call. Always share a specific window or tab, never the full screen, unless you truly mean to.

Summary (AI) — The note-taker bot's after-call write-up. Useful, shareable — and still a recording's child, so share it with the same care.

T

Transcript — The word-for-word text of a call, usually made by an AI transcription tool. Handy to search, occasionally awkward to read, easy to delete.

Transcription — The act of turning spoken words into written text in real time.

U

Unmute — Turning your microphone back on. Do it on purpose, by checking for the muted-mic symbol first.

V

Virtual background — The app replacing the room behind you with a picture or blur. Fun and harmless; a real, tidy background works just as well.

W

Waiting room — A lobby some meetings use: you arrive, the host lets you in. Not a demotion; just a doorbell.

WhatsApp — A messaging app whose calls handle both audio and video on phones. Popular in families that span countries; not great for bigger group meetings.

Z

Zoom — The video-calling app most workplaces and many clubs use, with the most settings we discuss in this guide (push to talk, captions, host controls, AI Companion). Free for calls up to 40 minutes; fine for almost every family visit.
